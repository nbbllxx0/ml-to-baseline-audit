# Re-test census of code-linked speed claims — pre-registration

**Version:** 1.0 · **Written:** 2026-09-25 · **Status:** frozen 2026-09-25 in `v3/evidence/MANIFEST.sha256`
(label `v3-census-prereg`), before any timed run; the user approved the budget rule. Changes go in §9 only.

**Why.** v2 re-tested two methods (OAT, HGTO). A reviewer can ask why those two. The census re-tests every speed claim
in the analysis sample whose article links public code, so the choice is not ours. The v2 submission is unchanged;
the census is for the revision (v3).

**Isolation.** Everything for the census lives in `v3/`. Files outside `v3/` are read, never written. Third-party
repositories go in `v3/third_party/<name>@<commit>/` and are not redistributed unless their licence allows it.

---

## 1. Frame

Speed-stratum records (analysis sample, adjudicated coding) with `reporting.code_available == "yes"`. The frame is
generated by `v3/tools/census_frame.py` into `v3/census/frame.md` and `frame.json`: 7 claims, of which P00150 (HGTO)
was re-tested in v2. The census re-tests the other 6: P00080, P00140, P00183, P00610, P00647, P00663.

No claim leaves the frame. A claim that cannot be re-run is reported with its outcome level (§3) and the exact blocker.

## 2. What is re-tested

For each claim, the **primary comparison** is the one the record codes as the claim (`claim.primary_outcome_paraphrase`
in `frame.json`). The published factor is the record's `claimed_speedup` with its stated basis.

## 3. Outcome levels (each claim reaches the highest level it can)

1. **L0 — runs.** The article's code runs on our machine and produces the article's ML output (or the classical
   output, when the ML side cannot run).
2. **L1 — same machine.** The article's own ML method and the article's own classical comparator, as published, are
   timed on our machine under §4. A comparator that the article projected instead of running is run, if its code is
   public; otherwise the projection is recomputed from our unit costs, and labelled as a projection.
3. **L2 — strong baseline.** The classical comparator's formulation stays fixed (objective, constraints, filter,
   penalty, update, stopping rule). Only the state solve and the device change, as in `plan/BASELINE_PREREG.md`
   (purpose paragraph). Time is compared at matched quality (§5).

Reported per claim: published factor; L1 factor; L2 factor at matched quality; the level reached; blockers.

## 4. Timing and hardware

As `plan/BASELINE_PREREG.md` §4–6: warm-up run discarded, three timed repeats, median with minimum and maximum; GPU
timers synchronized; setup reported separately. CPU runs are pinned to P-cores 0–11 (i5-14600KF). `tools/envcapture.py`
output is copied into `v3/env/`. Published times from other hardware are never mixed with ours in one ratio.

## 5. Matched quality

The article's own quality evaluation is used on both sides (compliance, effective property, or objective as the
article defines it). The ML output's evaluated quality is the target. The classical time is the time to the first
iteration that reaches it, with the same thresholding and post-processing on both sides. Where the article compares
at a fixed classical iteration count, that count is also reported.

## 6. Compute budget and failure rule

1. **Training budget: 1 GPU-hour per claim on our RTX 5090** (user, 2026-09-25). Released weights are used when they
   exist. Otherwise the model is retrained with the article's code and settings if data generation plus training
   finishes within 1 GPU-hour of measured wall time on our machine. A run that passes 1 GPU-hour is stopped; the ML
   side then keeps its published time, labelled "published".
2. **Permitted repairs to reach L0.** Install the stated or nearest available dependency versions; fix file paths and
   input/output; replace a removed library call with its documented equivalent. Every repair is logged in
   `v3/runs/<pid>/REPAIRS.md` with the diff.
3. **Blockers.** A claim stops at the level reached, with the blocker named, if going further would need (a) a file
   that is neither in the repository nor in an archive the article or repository links, or (b) a change to the
   numerical algorithm or its parameters.
4. **Long classical runs.** A classical run longer than 1 h is timed once, without a warm-up run; its variance is
   small against its length. All other runs follow §4.
